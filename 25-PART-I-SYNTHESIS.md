# 25 — Part I Synthesis: The YC Selection Model

## Executive conclusion
> **YC appears to favor unusually capable founders who can identify a real, important problem and use technology to attack it quickly, with a credible path to a very large outcome.**

AI is currently a major enabling layer, but AI itself is not the complete thesis.

## Evidence stack
```text
YC stated criteria
      ↓
RFS / partner signals
      ↓
Batch composition
      ↓
Founder research
      ↓
Success + failure cases
      ↓
Current portfolio
      ↓
Testable selection model
```

## The model
1. **Founder capability** — unusual ability to build, learn, reason or operate.
2. **Problem severity** — customers genuinely care.
3. **Insight** — founders understand something non-obvious.
4. **Technical leverage** — software, AI, robotics, data or another technical shift creates leverage where appropriate.
5. **Scale** — the initial wedge can become a very large company.
6. **Execution velocity** — the team can build, learn and adapt quickly.

### Technologist + Leverage + Scale
```text
Exceptional team
      ↓
Deep problem insight
      ↓
Technical / operational leverage
      ↓
Large-market wedge
      ↓
Rapid learning loop
      ↓
Venture-scale outcome
```

## Five Part-II hypotheses

### H1 — Founder capability
Stronger evidence of execution ability predicts investor interest after controlling for sector and batch.

### H2 — Problem leverage
Companies attacking expensive, repetitive or coordination-heavy workflows are disproportionately represented among major outcomes.

### H3 — AI leverage
AI is a stronger signal when it materially changes workflow economics rather than merely adding a feature.

### H4 — Early-stage potential
YC can select companies before conventional traction when founder/problem/insight signals are unusually strong.

### H5 — Scale
Large market opportunity is central to the venture model.

## The critical correction
Do not blur:
1. **Why was the company selected?**
2. **Why did it succeed/fail?**
3. **Which selection signals predicted the outcome?**

Only answering all three lets Venture Atlas claim to have reverse-engineered investor decision-making.

## Final status
Part I is a research-grade synthesis and hypothesis generator. It does not claim that YC's secret algorithm has been discovered.
