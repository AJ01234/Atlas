# Executive Summary

## What this project is
The Venture Atlas is a living research book about how top startup investors choose companies, and what those choices reveal about the market. The goal is not just to list startups, but to reverse-engineer the patterns behind investor decisions.

## Why it matters
Most startup advice focuses on ideas. Investors, however, usually evaluate a wider set of signals:
- founder quality
- market timing
- problem severity
- technical leverage
- distribution potential
- defensibility
- speed of execution

The Venture Atlas is designed to capture those signals in a structured way.

## How the Atlas is organized
The project is split into modular Markdown chapters so it can grow over time:

- project vision and documentation
- research methodology
- YC batch analysis
- company profiles
- investor comparisons
- market maps
- opportunity maps
- white-space analysis
- final synthesis

## What the first YC analysis chapter will do
The YC chapters will answer questions like:
- What kinds of startups are being funded most often?
- What sectors repeat across batches?
- What patterns show up in founders and products?
- What does YC seem to value right now?
- Which markets look crowded, and which still look open?

## Working principle
Each claim in the Atlas should be labeled as one of:
- **Official**
- **Verified**
- **Inference**

That keeps the document useful as both a reference and an analysis tool.

## What comes next
The next chapter should define the research method in a precise way:
- sources to use
- how to classify startups
- how to handle uncertainty
- how to distinguish fact from inference
- how to score opportunities

That methodology will set the standard for every chapter after it.
