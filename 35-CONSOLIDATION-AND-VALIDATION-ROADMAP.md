## What was consolidated conceptually

### Selection vs. outcome
The Atlas must distinguish:

1. Why an investor selected a company.
2. Why the company later succeeded or failed.
3. Which observable signals predict the outcome.

A success pattern is not automatically a selection signal, and a selection signal is not automatically causal.

### Stated thesis vs. revealed behavior
Investor websites and partner essays establish **stated philosophy**. Portfolio composition establishes **revealed behavior**. The two should be compared, not assumed identical.

### Technology layer vs. sector
AI, agents, data and robotics can cut across sectors. The Atlas therefore avoids treating AI as a mutually exclusive market category.

### White space vs. low activity
Low investor activity is not itself evidence of opportunity. White space requires a combination of unmet pain, market potential, timing, technology leverage, distribution and survivable constraints.

## Remaining validation work

The next major research phase is empirical rather than narrative.

### Dataset
Build a frozen, date-stamped company-level dataset containing, where available:
- investor / accelerator
- batch or investment date
- founders
- founder background variables
- founding-team size
- geography
- sector
- technology layer
- initial traction
- funding history
- major outcomes
- shutdown / inactive status

### Core tests

**H1 — Founder capability**  
Do execution/build signals predict selection or outcomes after controlling for sector and cohort?

**H2 — Problem leverage**  
Are expensive, repetitive, coordination-heavy workflows disproportionately represented among major outcomes?

**H3 — AI leverage**  
Does AI create stronger outcomes when it changes workflow economics rather than merely adding a feature?

**H4 — Early-stage judgment**  
Can elite investors identify winners before conventional traction is visible?

**H5 — Compounding advantage**  
Do network effects, proprietary data, workflow embedding, distribution or technical differentiation predict durability?

## Required controls

At minimum, separate:
- selection bias
- survivorship bias
- sector effects
- cohort effects
- geography effects
- funding-stage effects
- founder-background effects

## Falsification standard

Every major Atlas claim should have an explicit statement of what evidence would make us downgrade or reject it.

## Completion criterion

Venture Atlas becomes **empirically complete** only when its major selection claims have been tested against a company-level dataset and the results include both positive and negative findings.

Until then, the Atlas is a high-quality research synthesis and hypothesis engine—not a validated investment algorithm.
