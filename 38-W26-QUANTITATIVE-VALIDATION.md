# 38 — W26 Quantitative Validation

**Research cut-off:** 22 August 2026  
**Status:** empirical portfolio validation; not causal proof

## Evidence base

CB Insights analyzed all **199 W26 companies** and reports 15 categories, roughly **1 in 8** building something physical, **35** in Industrials & Defense, and **39** building AI infrastructure. citeturn5search0

Extruct reports **22 solo founders** in W26 and says 3× as many companies reached $1M annualized revenue as in W25. citeturn5search7

BatchPro's separate 196-company snapshot reports 58 Artificial Intelligence, 51 AI-focused, 42 B2B, 29 SaaS, 26 Developer Tools, 18 Fintech, 17 Infrastructure and 12 Robotics. Because its denominator and taxonomy differ, these figures are not merged with CB Insights. citeturn5search18

## Explicit calculations

### Physical companies

`1 / 8 = 12.5%`

So roughly 12.5% of W26 is building something physical. This supports a broadening of YC's technical substrate beyond software. citeturn5search0

### Industrials & Defense

`35 / 199 = 17.6%`

CB Insights says the category grew from 17 companies in the previous cohort to 35 in W26:

`(35 - 17) / 17 = 105.9%`

The company count therefore more than doubled. This is **not** a claim that category market share grew 106%, because the cited source does not provide the comparable prior-cohort denominator. citeturn5search0

### AI infrastructure

`39 / 199 = 19.6%`

AI infrastructure is therefore one of the most visible single themes in W26. It does **not** mean 19.6% of YC is AI; categories overlap. citeturn5search0

### Solo founders

`22 / 199 = 11.1%`

This disproves the simplistic claim that YC only backs two-founder teams, but it does not establish equal outcome probabilities for solo founders. citeturn5search7

## Hypothesis results

### H3 — AI leverage
**STRONGLY SUPPORTED as a current portfolio pattern.**

AI is functioning as a broad technical substrate rather than a narrow sector label. This is consistent with the W26 composition and YC's 2026 RFS emphasis on AI-native workflows. citeturn5search0

### H2 — Problem leverage
**DIRECTIONALLY SUPPORTED.**

The cohort repeatedly attacks operational bottlenecks in AI infrastructure, industrial automation, robotics, healthcare, compliance, finance and enterprise workflows. That is consistent with the painful-workflow hypothesis, but does not prove that pain caused selection.

Alternative explanations include founder supply, technical feasibility and market timing.

### H5 — Venture scale
**STRUCTURALLY SUPPORTED, NOT CAUSALLY VALIDATED.**

The physical/industrial shift shows YC is willing to underwrite technically difficult markets. It does not prove those companies will become large outcomes.

## The strongest current pattern

The evidence is better represented as a stack than as “YC = AI”:

```text
AI / New Models
      ↓
Infrastructure / Tools
      ↓
Industry-specific execution
      ↓
Physical / real-world systems
```

Current YC company pages illustrate the same progression: on-device AI infrastructure, AI infrastructure optimization, industrial robotics, healthcare agents and enterprise workflow systems. citeturn5search9turn5search10turn5search5

### Inference

YC's current opportunity map appears less about “AI apps” and more about **where intelligence becomes economically useful**.

## What this changes in the selection model

Earlier model:

> Founder capability + problem severity + insight + technical leverage + timing + scale.

Refined model:

> **Founder capability × painful problem × newly available capability × economic translation × scale**

Economic translation means the technology should do at least one of the following:

- reduce cost;
- increase throughput;
- remove labor from a workflow;
- make a previously impractical workflow possible;
- create a new market;
- generate proprietary feedback/data.

This is a stronger model than **AI + large TAM**.

## Critical limitations

1. **Different denominators:** CB Insights uses 199; BatchPro uses 196.
2. **Overlapping tags:** AI, B2B, infrastructure, robotics and healthcare are not mutually exclusive.
3. **Selection vs supply:** portfolio composition cannot distinguish YC preference from founder supply without application-level data.
4. **Young cohort:** W26 is too young for long-run outcome analysis.
5. **No acceptance-time causal test yet:** we still need the full company-level panel specified in Chapter 37.

## Bottom line

> **The strongest current YC signal is not “AI.” It is the combination of a capable technical team, a newly available capability, a consequential workflow or physical problem, and a credible path to turning that capability into large economic leverage.**

This is now an evidence-backed hypothesis. It is **not yet a statistically validated prediction model**.
